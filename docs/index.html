50 W power supply — project notes

POWER ELECTRONICS / FEB — MAY 2026

Design, simulate, build, compare.

The aim was to build a 50 W dual-switch forward converter and compare how two feedback compensators handled output ripple. This was a university team project; the work below describes my contributions.

What I worked on

  • Calculated transformer ratio, output filter values, duty cycle, and component ratings.
  • Built ideal, non-ideal, and closed-loop PLECS models, comparing a basic integral controller with an advanced compensator.
  • Designed a two-layer KiCad PCB and hand-wound the main transformer, driver transformer, and output inductor.
  • Tested the PWM drive and compensation networks at full and light loads, and assessed thermal behaviour using simulation and infrared measurements.

What the results showed

In simulation, the advanced compensator reduced 100 Hz low-frequency ripple from approximately 0.5 V to 0.12 V. Hardware tests confirmed the same trend: lower ripple with advanced compensation under AC input.

The practical connection: control design, magnetic components, PCB layout, and thermal behaviour all influence the same output.

Doppler radar speedometer — project notes

SENSING & EMBEDDED SYSTEMS / SEP 2025 — JAN 2026

From a small signal to a useful readout.

A university team project that took the output of an HB100 Doppler radar through analog conditioning, digital frequency extraction, and a numerical speed display.

What I worked on

  • Designed an OP07-based active bandpass filter with a target passband of 78–1169 Hz, and an MCP6002 Schmitt comparator for waveform shaping.
  • Developed STM32U575 firmware for timer-triggered ADC sampling and CMSIS-DSP FFT analysis.
  • Wrote Verilog for an XC2C64A CPLD, including UART reception, clock division, and seven-segment display decoding.
  • Integrated the analog and digital stages and tested with hand-swept and moving targets.

What the results showed

The integrated system detected moving targets and displayed real-time speed values. The project validated the complete signal chain, from a weak radar return to a digital readout.

The practical connection: useful sensing depends on the entire chain, from analog signal quality to firmware and display logic.

Embedded smart vehicle — project notes

EMBEDDED SYSTEMS / SEP 2024 — MAY 2025

Many small systems. One moving platform.

A university team project built around Arduino Nano and Raspberry Pi, progressing from a basic four-wheel platform to vision-assisted navigation and multi-task control.

What I worked on

  • Assembled the platform and implemented H-bridge motor drive, encoder feedback, I²C communication, and a local control interface.
  • Integrated RF and Bluetooth remote control, along with an MPU6050 motion sensor.
  • Developed PID line following using infrared sensors and an eight-sensor weighted-average approach.
  • Used OpenCV for colour-based path recognition and task-board identification, with a state machine to manage task execution and ultrasonic sensing for parking.

What the results showed

The vehicle completed line following, colour-triggered responses, integrated tasks, and autonomous parking. Testing involved both hardware debugging and control-algorithm tuning.

The practical connection: reliable autonomy comes from integrating sensing, control, communication, and clear system states.
